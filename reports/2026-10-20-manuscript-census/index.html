How many manuscript staves we have, and how many are labelled

Every manuscript and autograph of the quartets in the scan archive, outside Op. 48. Staves are counted with the labeler's own staff finder, so the numbers match what it would propose.

Why "paired" isn't "labelled". A manuscript counts as paired when its staves were matched to an encoding bar by bar. Only the staves that matched cleanly are kept: 2,445 of the 4,884 in dataM's manuscripts, 372 of Madrid's 678, 99 of KHM 587's 236. That is why the paired manuscripts hold about 5,800 staves but Boccherini 3k has 3,411: its 2,817 kept staves from these, plus 595 from Op. 48.

How the staves were counted
  • The labeler's staff finder and its empty-staff test, on 2,800 px renders: the detection the labeler proposes.
  • Four rules on top: scanner calibration cards aren't music; a page with one inked staff is a title page; a page with little ink on its staves is a title page; a faint staff under a heading is a heading line.
  • About 60 pages corrected by eye. Nine documents counted by eye instead.
  • Against Op. 48's 888 labelled staves, it counts 907 (+2.1%) and no title pages. On KHM 587 the finder undercounts: page 15 has 10 staves, not 7.
Doubts and exclusions
  • US-Wc "Op. 54" is about 1,350 staves, ±10%, from 85 photos of two-page openings with 8 title openings: probably several part-books. Its G. 250–255 numbering is the folder's name, unverified.
  • The two 20-page CZ-Pu PDFs hold the same pages; counted once. MS-200's image 14 repeats image 13.
  • Left out: S-Skma W 4-R, catalogued as a manuscript but the Hummel print; a BnF item marked not a quartet; Op. 48 (G. 226–231); all prints.
