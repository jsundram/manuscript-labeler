Reading the numbers

The denominator is what detection found, not the staves on the page. It counts empty staves picked up as music and boxes that straddle two staves, both common on the Op. 32 pages. The yield on real music staves is somewhat higher.

Detection is where staves are lost. Its staff and bar-line detectors were trained on the Op. 48 sources only. On the Op. 32 hand, with thin, short bar lines and ten staves to a landscape page, it misses most bar lines and misplaces some staves.

The new rule: a staff no longer needs detection's bar count to match Zeus's. When they differ, its first and last measures must each pair with a single bar. On the three labelled Op. 48 sources this kept 304 staves with none wrong. It was designed around the one failure it removed, so it is lightly tested.

check marks sources where detection found almost no bar lines, so nearly every staff got in through the new rule alone. Leave them out of the first training run, or spot-check a few first.

Skipped: engraved prints, for now; the Op. 24 autograph, whose hand is unlike the others; and the Stams pages, three fragments whose numbering disagrees.

Held out for testing: KHM 602 movement II and Vma ms 1067 (1) movement II stay out of training.
