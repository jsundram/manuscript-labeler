MuSViT readers D4 and D3 (musvit-D4, 2026-10-20; musvit-D3, 2026-10-18)zeus-score's default since 1.2.0

zeus-score (this card in Markdown) · MuSViT (PRAIG) · experiments/musvit (the reader, training, results) · Zeus J's card

A reader for Boccherini's copyists built on MuSViT, an image encoder pretrained on 9.7 million pages of printed music. It reads one staff at a time and writes out its notes in Zeus's output format, so the edition and zeus-score can use it in Zeus's place. On the clean test it gets 270 of 347 bars exact against Zeus J's 225. It was zeus-score's default from 1.2.0. Its flags for the edition, measured at the same floor, are as good as Zeus J's with a third fewer false ones.

musvit-D4, the default since 1.3.0, is D3's recipe with two changes: staves read 96 px high instead of 128, and a stage of 10,790 synthetic staves drawn in the copyists' hands between the public staves and ours. It gets 277 of 347 bars exact (80%) and 92.9% of events right (D3: 91.6%); wrong pitches fell from 45 to 25. Against D3 that is +7 bars, with a 95% interval of −5 to +19: as good, not shown to be better. It reads about 30% faster. Its flags for the edition haven't been re-measured. How it was chosen: reports/2026-10-20-synthetic-and-strip-height. The rest of this card describes D3; D4 shares its data and training but for those two changes.

The model

ArchitectureMuSViT's encoder (a ViT-Base: 12 layers, width 768, 16 px patches, 89M parameters) reads a staff image 128 px high at its own width (up to 2,688 px), as 8 rows of patches. An 8-layer transformer decoder (width 256, 8 heads) attends to every patch and writes the staff as tokens. 98M parameters in all; 15 times Zeus's size.
OutputLinearized MusicXML (LMX), Zeus's format: 137 tokens in all (the public staves' and ours), including two voices written as Zeus's data writes them (line 1, <backup>, line 2).
Starting weightsMuSViT (PRAIG/musvit on Hugging Face, ECCV 2026), MAE-pretrained on IMSLP pages
This modelD3-tf8-all, pass 24 of a 30-pass schedule. 195 MB (half precision, its adapters folded into the encoder). sha256 of reader.safetensors in the registry.
How MuSViT is usedAs its model card advises for fine-tuning: loaded as a plain ViTModel (not AutoModel, which would hide 70% of the patches), position embeddings interpolated to each staff's size, pixels 0–1 with no normalisation, grey copied to three channels. Its pages were pretrained at 1024 × 1024, so a staff here is drawn larger than it was then.
InputThe staff Zeus is given: compare.py's crop from the bar export (a staff space beyond each end, 2.5 above and below, straightened, the paper brightened), saved as JPEG at quality 95, then resized to 128 px high.
EnvironmentPython 3.10–3.12, PyTorch, transformers, safetensors. No MuSViT download and no peft: the archive holds the whole reader. On a Mac it reads on the GPU (mps) in half precision.
TrainingOn Modal's GPUs: stage 1 about an hour on an L40S, stage 2 about 22 minutes.
CodeThe labeler's experiments/musvit/: proto.py (the reader, training, reading), modal_app.py (Modal), read.py (the clean test), release.py (this archive); tools/musvit_score.py (scoring, as tools/zeus_score.py).

How it was trained

  • Stage 1 (run E2-tf8): the decoder and rank-16 LoRA adapters on the encoder's attention, trained on the public staves; best pass chosen on Dolores staves.
  • Stage 2: stage 1's adapters folded into the encoder and fresh rank-64 adapters on its attention and MLP layers. Learning rate 1e-4 with warm-up and cosine decay over 30 passes, batch 8, bfloat16, a sideways shift of up to 8 px. D1 was scored every 3 passes on a practice exam (KHM 602 II and Op. 48 No. 5 I in both sources, 147 staves); its best was pass 24. D3 repeats the recipe with the practice exam in training and stops at pass 24.
  • What mattered (each on the clean test): the full MuSViT over the light one; larger adapters (+3.2 points); an 8-layer decoder over a 4-layer one (+6.6); Boccherini's other copyists' staves. What didn't: Zeus's augmentation, unlocking the encoder's top layers, beam search, LSTM and Zeus-style decoders, column memory, and continued pretraining on our manuscripts. The last two helped alone but not together with the 8-layer decoder.

Unseen hands and unseen music

The clean test's copyist is in training (KHM 602, the same hand), so it can't say how the reader does on a hand it has never seen. The Madrid copies of Op. 22 (372 staves paired automatically) are such a hand. But Op. 22's music is in training through other copies, and that matters more than expected.   • Knowing the music halves the errors at equal data (11.6% against 24.1%). A reader leans on the notes it has learned. Every Madrid number above, Zeus J's included, is flattered by it.
  • On a work and a hand it has never seen, expect about a quarter of symbols wrong. No missing vocabulary explains it: every Madrid token is in that reader's training data.
  • For the edition this is mostly good news: most sources are copies of works already encoded somewhere, which is the Madrid case.

What fine-tuning gave up: the public benchmarks

MusiCorpus's test staves, which no model trained on: Dolores (2,357 staves) and OmniOMR (257). Every model read them the same way and was scored the same way: symbol edits over the reference's length, readings cut at twice the reference's length (Zeus loops past a staff's end on a few). "On our symbols" leaves out of both sides the tokens our labels never contain (about 10%: stem directions, hidden header marks, tremolos, accents), which a reader trained on our staves can't learn to write. No paper reports a number for Zeus's solo-staff snapshot, so stock Zeus is scored here too.

  • Before fine-tuning, the MuSViT reader starts where stock Zeus does, a little ahead (17.2% against 18.2% on Dolores).
  • Fine-tuning on Boccherini costs both a lot on these staves: their errors roughly double. Both learned our copyists' habits (an alto clef expected, Boccherini's figures) and drift on other music. Zeus's own fine-tunes did the same.
  • D3 gives up less than Zeus J: 32.8% against 39.2% on Dolores, 38.8% against 48.7% on OmniOMR. It is better than Zeus J on its own task and forgets less of the general one.
  • For general handwritten music, use stock Zeus or a stage-1 reader, not these.

Cached decoding gives the same readings; half precision changed 1 staff of 64, and the clean test came out at 265 bars on the Mac against 263 on Modal's GPU. The encoder is now near the Mac GPU's limit (about 0.16 s a staff): the reader is 5½ times slower than Zeus J at reading, because it is 15 times bigger. For scoring it doesn't matter.

As the edition's second reader

Measured as Zeus J's card measures it: on Op. 48 No. 2 I as it stood right after the read, before any correction (neither model trained on it). 107 of its 337 bar-parts were later corrected in the kinds the reader checks (pitch, rhythm, accidentals). A bar-part is flagged where the reader finds its own token likelier than ours by more than the floor, in log-probability (the edition's differences.objections).

  • The floor stays at 3. D3's flags change little from 2 to 6 (121 to 108 flagged, 96 to 92 right): it is surer of itself than Zeus J, whose flags fall away above 4. At 8 it still catches 85% of the errors (Zeus J 59%).
  • Against Zeus J: a third fewer false flags (21 against 30) for one error fewer caught; better on rhythm, a little worse on pitch. On 107 errors in one movement, differences of two or three are within chance.
  • Both together (flag only where both object) cut false flags to 14 and lose nothing more, at the cost of running two readers.
  • A bias in Zeus J's favour: the corrections were found partly by following Zeus J's flags, so errors it missed are likelier to be missing from the truth.

Known limits

  • New hands: about 24% symbol errors on a hand and a work it hasn't seen.
  • Rhythm in dense passages: dropped or added groups of 32nds, dots.
  • Public staves: see above; it is a Boccherini specialist now.
  • Speed: 0.28 s a staff to read on a Mac, against Zeus J's 0.05.
  • A small clean test: one movement can't separate readers within about 4 points.

Future directions

  • Synthetic quartet staves, printed then handwritten, aimed at new hands (experiments/musvit/NEXT.md).
  • A second clean test in a new hand, so that progress on new hands can be measured without the music flattering it.
  • The edition's switch: zeus-score 1.2.0 makes it the default; the edition takes it when it upgrades (floor 3, measured above). Zeus J remains the more independent reader for Op. 48 No. 5, which D3 trained on.
