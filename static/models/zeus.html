Zeus, run Jzeus-score's default until 1.2.0; now musvit-D3

OmniOMR's reader for handwritten music, fine-tuned on Boccherini's copyists. It reads one staff at a time and writes out its notes. The edition uses it as a second reader: where Zeus strongly disagrees with an encoded note, the encoding checks raise a flag.

zeus-score (the package: install, inputs, outputs; this card in Markdown) · Zeus (OmniOMR) · experiments/zeus (training data, fine-tuning, scoring) · tools/zeus_score.py (what the edition calls)

Model card

Architecture
Zeus "solo26": a small convolutional network (3 stages of 2 residual blocks, 32 channels) reads a staff image 96 px high. Each 16 px-wide slice goes to a 2-layer recurrent encoder (192 units), and a 1-layer decoder writes the staff as a sequence of tokens.
Output
Linearized MusicXML (LMX) tokens, 168 in all: measures, clefs, keys, times, pitches, accidentals, durations, dots, beams, slurs, ties, chords, rests, grace notes, fermatas. Pitches are named from where the head sits in Zeus's own clef. Our scoring renames them in the clef the labels give, because Zeus's clefs are often wrong on these hands.
Starting weights
ayce-2026-08-03, OmniOMR's solo-staff snapshot, trained on MusiCorpus (Dolores train and validation, OmniOMR train)
This model
J-lr1e5-e8, version 2026-10-16: run J's eighth and last epoch (28 MB). Published as a release; sha256 of weights.h5 39eada41…
Licence
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, inherited from Zeus's weights: non-commercial use, share alike
Input
One staff cut from the bar export: from its left end to its right, a staff space beyond each, 2.5 spaces above and below, straightened along its bend, the paper brightened to white. A staff space is about 11 px.
Where it runs
The zeus-score package (zeus-score/): zeus-score fetch downloads the model and checks its hash, and zeus-score models lists what each model was trained on. It carries tools/zeus_score.py, which the edition's encode.py checks runs for every encoded movement. experiments/zeus/compare.py does the scoring on this card.
Environment
Python 3.10 and tensorflow 2.12 in ~/.cache/manuscript-labeler/zeus/py. It runs outside Claude Code's sandbox.
Training time
2.5 hours on this Mac: 8 epochs of 321 steps. The first two epochs ran at background priority and took 33 and 44 minutes; the rest ran at full speed and took 10 to 14 minutes each.
Adopted
2026-10-16, replacing run G

How training works

  • Recipe (since run D): Zeus's own trainer continues from the stock weights. Learning rate 1e-5 with cosine decay to 0, batch 8, 8 epochs, seed 42. The only augmentation is a sideways shift of up to 8 px. The last epoch is kept; no epoch is chosen on test scores.
  • Why so gentle. The first fine-tunes, at 1e-4 for 20 to 30 epochs, learned the Berlin copyist and forgot the Paris hands: RES 507 (14) II went from 83% to 39% of bars exact. At 1e-5, hands it trained on gain and unseen hands don't lose. Neither the full augmentation set nor more epochs helped.
  • Built by experiments/zeus/dataset.py (Op. 48 staves from the labels' bar export and the encoding), autodetect.py and autopair.py (Philhar staves), and finetune.py (Zeus's train).

  • Where it fails: dense 32nd notes. About 90 of the viola's 104 wrong pitches are in one broken-chord figure (bars 26–31 and its return in 61–67, C4 E4 A3 E4 / B3 D4 E3 D4). Zeus reads the upper notes but pulls the low string's note up toward its neighbours. The clefs were read right. The violins' passagework loses bars to duration errors in the same way.
  • Where it's good: the cello, at 91% exact, and sparse writing in general.

As the edition's second reader

The encoding checks score each encoded bar with Zeus. An objection to a pitch, a rhythm or an accidental becomes a flag when Zeus finds its own reading more likely than ours by more than 3 in log-probability (the floor). This was measured on Op. 48 No. 2 I as it stood right after the read, before any correction. J never saw that movement. 107 of its 337 bar-parts were later corrected in a kind Zeus flags.

  • The floor stays at 3. Floors from 2.5 to 4 catch the same wrong bars, give or take one or two. At 4, J flags 114 bar-parts and 95 of them are wrong.
  • A rule fixed while re-tuning. Objections were dropped whenever Zeus's preferred token was a beam. J often prefers a beam where we wrote an accidental, which means it wants no accidental there. Without the fix, J flagged only 25 of the 80 wrong accidentals and G only 41. With it, both flag 78.
  • Rhythm is the weak spot. Only 10 of 27 rhythm corrections get flagged; LilyPond's bar check catches most of the rest.
  • Movements J trained on (every encoded Op. 48 movement but No. 2 I) will draw fewer objections, because it has learned the edition's reading of them.

The family, stock to J

Every model fine-tuned so far. The development movements guided the recipe and flatter the models (KHM 602 II overstates by about 20 points), so the clean test is the fair column. All of them were scored on it on 2026-10-16.

What the clean test shows: the recipe work, from the first fine-tune to D, didn't move it (47–48%); it kept unseen hands from getting worse. The big step came with E, from Boccherini's other copyists: 13 points. After that, gains are a few points a run, near the test's noise. Known limits

  • Dense passagework. 32nd notes, and broken chords that cross strings: durations are misread, and low notes are pulled toward their neighbours.
  • Double stops come out as two voices, often with different rhythms. Two-voice staves are kept out of training, so it has never learned ours.
  • Clefs. Its clef tokens are unreliable. Its note placement is fine, so scoring renames pitches in the labels' clef. Forcing the labelled clef made the viola worse (clefread.py).
  • Hands it hasn't seen. Gains are largest on trained hands. Op. 48 Nos. 4–6 (Vma ms 1067 (2), RES 507 (16) and (17)) are new hands until some of their staves are encoded.
  • Resolution. It sees about 11 px per staff space, so a head misplaced by a step is hard for it to see.
  • A small clean test. 347 bars from one copyist can't tell runs apart within about 4 points.

Future directions

  • Encode a few staves of each new hand. This has been the one reliable lever: Op. 48 No. 3 I moved Vma II 5 points when thousands of other staves hadn't. The first movement of each of Op. 48 Nos. 4–6, encoded and checked, would teach Zeus those copyists. The movement after it can serve as that hand's test.
  • A bigger clean test, one per hand. Hold one movement of each hand out for good. That would let runs be told apart within a point or two, and make seeds or ensembles worth measuring.
  • Two-voice staves and double stops. Write them as chords or voices in the training data instead of leaving them out. That addresses most of the 35 chord-size errors and some of the extra events.
  • Rhythm, decoded under the bar check. Duration is the largest error. A search over Zeus's readings that only accepts measures adding up to the time signature would fix most of what the bar check now only flags.
  • Pitch, measured rather than re-read. Where Zeus's step is doubtful, measure the head's place on the staff (headcheck, the aligner) instead of asking Zeus again.
  • Passagework. Oversample dense 32nd-note bars, or render the input taller (more than 96 px a staff) so heads in fast figures are better separated. Both mean a new run; a taller input means training from more than our data alone.
  • More Philhar staves. 37% of those sources' staves are still unpaired, and the Op. 24 autograph full score needs whole systems paired. Bar-line detection is no longer what limits this.

Terms

Bars exact
The share of bars whose every event (note, rest, chord, grace) matches the encoding in pitch and duration. Marks such as slurs, dynamics and beams aren't counted.
Right in rhythm, in pitch
Bars whose durations all match, or whose pitches all match, whatever the other is.
Clean test
KHM 603 movement I: never trained on, and never used to choose anything.
Development movements
Movements held out of a run's training and watched while choosing the recipe. They flatter the models that were chosen by them.
Floor
How much more likely, in log-probability, Zeus must find its own token than ours before the encoding checks flag the bar.
Philhar
Encodings of Boccherini's quartets made from his autographs (boccherini-quartet-data). Their manuscripts are copyists' parts, paired with the encodings automatically.
